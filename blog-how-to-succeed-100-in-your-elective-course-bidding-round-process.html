Elective course bidding

How to succeed in your elective course bidding round: a student’s guide

Course bidding tips for students: plan bid points, use the practice round, read the MRB, add a buffer and use Add/Drop and waitlists. Based on Registro rounds.

By Arun Korupolu

The short answer: preparation matters more than luck. Before the round, know your credit rules and rank your electives into must-haves and backups. Use the practice round to read demand. During the live round, watch the clearing price (MRB), keep a buffer on your most popular choices and stay alert in the final minutes. No strategy can guarantee a seat, but a clear plan gives you the best chance of building the elective portfolio you want.

A note before you start: this guide offers high-level advice based on inputs from students who have taken part in course bidding on the Registro platform. Every institution sets its own rules, and every cohort bids differently, so develop your own strategy from what you see in your practice round.

What should you do before the bidding round opens?

Most bidding mistakes are made before the round starts, not during it. Preparation and planning are what separate a calm round from a frantic one.

Know the rules of your programme

Read your programme office's bidding policy carefully. Check your minimum and maximum credits, any concentration or specialisation requirements, the size of your bid-point budget, whether unused points carry over to later terms and whether there is a minimum bid per course. These rules decide what a good outcome looks like for you.

Browse the catalogue, prerequisites and clashes

Registro gives you access to the course catalogue, course prerequisite information and course clashing information. Use all three. There is no point winning an elective you are not eligible for, or two electives that run in the same slot.

Sort your electives into tiers

Split your list into three groups: must-have electives you will fight for, electives you would like, and backup electives you would be happy to take. This makes every later decision faster, especially when demand moves quickly in the last minutes of a round.

Take the practice round seriously

Participate in the practice bidding round. It shows you the likely demand for each elective, lets you test basic bidding strategies and helps you identify backup courses. Treat it as a rehearsal, not a formality.

How do open and closed rounds change your strategy?

In an open round, you see real-time demand and can adjust your bids while the round is live. In a closed round, you submit sealed bids without seeing what others have placed. For a full comparison, see what is elective course bidding.

  • Open round: your main advantage is information. Watch demand, respond to changes and time your final adjustments.
  • Closed round: you get no feedback, so your practice-round observations and your tiers matter even more. Bid decisively on must-haves and keep your backups realistic.

How do you read demand during a live round?

Registro provides real-time insights to help you make informed decisions while the round is open:

  • Active bidders: see how many fellow students are bidding for each elective.
  • Clearing price: view the real-time computed clearing bid price for each elective.
  • Status updates: check whether you are currently winning or losing each elective.
  • Bid updates: change your bids during the round to hold a winning position.
  • Points used: track how many of your bid points you have committed.

Understand the MRB

The clearing bid price is shown as the MRB, the Minimum Required Bid. It is dynamic and can rise during an active round as more students bid. To win a seat at any given moment, your bid must be equal to or greater than the MRB. A winning status early in the round is not a winning result at the end of it.

Add a buffer on high-demand electives

For popular electives, consider placing bid points at least 20% to 30% above the current MRB. For example, if the MRB is 55, placing 70 points gives you a buffer and reduces the risk of being outbid. Use this for your must-haves, not for every course, or you will run short of points.

Watch the final minutes

Demand for popular electives often increases in the last minutes of an active round. Stay logged in, watch your status and be ready to adjust your points to keep your winning position on the courses that matter most.

How should you spread your bid points?

Diversify your elective portfolio. Mix high-demand electives with electives you can win for fewer points, so you secure the credits you need even if one or two popular courses go beyond your budget.

  • Put your largest bids on your must-have tier.
  • Keep some points in reserve for final-minute adjustments.
  • Do not overpay for low-demand courses. If demand is below the seats available, the MRB can stay low.
  • Recheck your total credits before the round closes.

What happens after the round: confirmations, Add/Drop and waitlists?

Registro confirms your winning seats after the round closes, so you know your result immediately rather than waiting for an email days later. If a must-have course did not come through, the round after bidding is your next chance.

Many institutions run Add/Drop rounds and waitlists for exactly this reason. At NMIMS Mumbai, Registro supports multiple elective bidding rounds and Add/Drop rounds in each trimester, and students on elective waitlists are moved into available seats automatically, without manual intervention. Read the NMIMS case study.

These processes are well tested. IIM Calcutta has delivered 21 term-level elective bidding cycles on Registro since 2017, including structured Add/Drop rounds, for more than 3,000 students, with satisfaction consistently above 4.5 out of 5. Read the IIM Calcutta case study.

How do you avoid technical problems on the day?

  • Use a reliable connection: bid from a stable internet connection, not a patchy mobile hotspot.
  • Use a supported browser: use a popular browser such as Google Chrome, and disable ad blockers when you access the Registro course bidding portal.
  • Use a laptop: a laptop or PC makes it easier to update bids and manage your courses than a small screen.
  • Contact support immediately: if something goes wrong, reach out to the Registro support team straight away, even during an active round.

The platform handles large cohorts. At OP Jindal Global University, more than 5,000 students bid in a single real-time window with complete visibility into the allocation process. Read the OPJGU case study.

What are the most common mistakes to avoid?

  1. Skipping the practice round and bidding blind on the day.
  2. Putting almost all your points on one elective with no backups.
  3. Bidding exactly the MRB on a popular course and walking away.
  4. Logging off before the final minutes of an open round.
  5. Ignoring clashes, prerequisites or credit limits until after results.

Frequently asked questions

Can any strategy guarantee a seat in a popular elective?

No. Seats go to the highest bids, and demand can change until the round closes. A good strategy improves your chances; it cannot promise a result.

What is the MRB in course bidding?

MRB stands for Minimum Required Bid. It is the live clearing price for an elective: the number of points you need to hold a seat at that moment. It can rise as demand grows.

How much above the MRB should I bid?

For high-demand electives, a buffer of 20% to 30% above the MRB reduces the risk of being outbid. For example, bid 70 points when the MRB is 55.

What should I do if I lose a must-have elective?

Use the Add/Drop round or join the waitlist if your institution offers one. On platforms with automatic waitlist movement, you are moved into an available seat without having to chase the programme office.

Do unused bid points carry over to the next term?

It depends on your institution's policy. Check it before the round, because it changes how aggressively you should spend.
