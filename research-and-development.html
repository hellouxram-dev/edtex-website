Research and development

Academic operations is an allocation problem

Building a multi-programme timetable, running an elective bidding round, slotting Day 0 interviews and placing students with partner universities are the same class of problem: allocating scarce resources against competing preferences and hard constraints.

EDTEX research treats academic operations as an allocation problem. Timetabling, elective bidding, Day 0 interview scheduling and student exchange are NP-hard problems, and EDTEX develops hybrid optimisation methods for them that run inside EDTEX Registro and EDTEX Skynet at 40+ higher education institutions.

What does an allocation problem look like?

A timetable is a good example. Every class needs a slot, a room and a teacher, no two classes can share any of them, and faculty preferences should be met wherever possible. Watch a solver resolve the clashes. Our consultants and engineers formulate each problem with your office, inside live bidding windows, timetable freezes and placement days, where the real constraints surface.

  1. Formulate the problemDecision variables, hard constraints and an objective, before any interface is built.
  2. Find the clashesTwo classes for one teacher or one room in the same slot break a hard constraint.
  3. Search for a better scheduleThe solver moves classes into free slots, keeping faculty preferences where it can.
  4. Benchmark on your historyThe result is compared with your own past terms before anything goes live.

Questions about EDTEX research

What kind of problems does EDTEX research?

Allocation problems in academic operations: multi-programme timetables, elective bidding rounds, Day 0 interview slots and student exchange placements. Most of these are NP-hard.

Which methods does EDTEX use for timetabling?

Hybrid optimisation methods drawing on combinatorial optimisation, constraint programming, integer linear programming and metaheuristics, producing conflict-free, preference-aware schedules across thousands of variables.

How does EDTEX test a new solver?

New solvers are benchmarked on the institution’s own past terms and cycles, so improvement is measured against what its office actually produced rather than a synthetic dataset.

Where is this research used?

Inside EDTEX Registro (course bidding, timetabling and student exchange) and EDTEX Skynet (placements) at 40+ institutions, including IIM Ahmedabad, IIM Bangalore, ISB and IIT Bombay.
