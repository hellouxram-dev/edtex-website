IIM Lucknow timetabling: finalisation time halved, 90% of clashes resolved automatically

Overview

IIM Lucknow implemented Registro Timetabling to manage the growing complexity of conflicts after course registration. The system resolved more than 90% of clashes automatically by moving students between sections, cut the time to finalise the timetable by 50% and improved classroom utilisation by 20%.

As the student body grew and course offerings expanded, overlapping enrolments, limited faculty availability and unbalanced sections caused friction for everyone. Registro was deployed to handle this end to end, from importing registration data and detecting clashes to publishing an optimised timetable and adjusting it for late changes.

More on the product: Registro Timetabling.

What was breaking down

As student numbers grew and courses multiplied, building clash-free timetables after course registration became unmanageable by hand. Students often enrolled in overlapping courses, administrators had no tools to detect conflicts early, and resolving clashes manually was slow and error-prone.

Multi-section courses made it harder. Students could in principle be moved to another section to avoid a clash, but there was no automated way to do this at scale while respecting room capacities and faculty preferences.

Where the friction lived

  • Students enrolling in overlapping courses with no early warning
  • Administrators unable to see clash patterns across the whole student body
  • Multi-section courses under-used, with no automatic movement of students between sections
  • Manual scheduling unable to balance rooms, faculty preferences and course loads together
  • Timetable finalisation taking too long because of manual conflict resolution
  • Last-minute enrolment changes disrupting the published timetable

How Registro fixed it

Registro Timetabling was connected to IIM Lucknow’s course registration platform to import enrolment data in real time and apply constraint-based optimisation across the scheduling process. Implementation followed four phases.

Early clash detection

A colour-coded dashboard lets students and administrators see conflicts before the timetable is final, and request changes.

Automatic section shuffling

Multi-section enrolments analysed and students moved to conflict-free sections, within capacities and faculty preferences.

Constraint-based optimisation

Integer programming and greedy algorithms balance clashes, classroom use, faculty preferences and course loads.

Dynamic adjustment

Published timetables adjusted for last-minute enrolment changes with minimal disruption.
