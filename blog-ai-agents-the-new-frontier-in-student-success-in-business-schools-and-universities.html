What can AI agents do for student success in business schools and universities?

AI agents in student information systems can answer routine student questions at any hour, remind students before deadlines, guide them through registration and career choices, and alert advisors when a student may need help.

By EDTEX

On this page

  2. What can AI agents do for students?
  3. Why do AI agents depend on clean operational data?
  4. What are the risks, and how should institutions manage them?
  5. How should a business school start?
  6. Frequently asked questions
  7. Conclusion

The short answer: AI agents in student information systems can answer routine student questions at any hour, remind students before deadlines, guide them through registration and career choices, and alert advisors when a student may need help. They work only as well as the data underneath them: an agent cannot explain a timetable clash if the timetable lives in a spreadsheet. Used with clear limits on data privacy, human oversight and bias, they extend the reach of programme offices rather than replace them.

This article explains what an AI agent in a student system is, what it can do for students, why clean operational data comes first, and the cautions every business school and university should plan for.

What is an AI agent in a student information system?

A chatbot answers questions from a fixed script. An AI agent goes further. It can look up a student's own record, understand the rules of a process, and suggest or take the next step within limits the institution sets. Ask a chatbot "when does add/drop close?" and it returns a date. Ask an agent "can I still drop this elective?" and it can check the student's registration, the credit policy and the calendar, and give an answer that applies to that student.

That difference is why agents are attracting attention in higher education. Much of what students ask the programme office is personal but routine: it depends on their record, yet the answer follows a rule.

What can AI agents do for students?

Answer questions at any hour

Students do not keep office hours. The question about degree requirements comes up late at night, and the question about bid points comes up on the evening before the window closes. An agent available 24/7 can give an immediate answer to common queries, and route the rest to a person.

Picture a programme office in the first two weeks of term. The same handful of questions arrive by email, phone and in person, hundreds of times over. Much of that volume is caused by a lack of visibility, not a lack of staff. At OP Jindal Global University, student queries during registration fell by 99% once bidding moved to a platform that gave students full visibility of the allocation process. Read the OPJGU case study. That result came from transparent rules and live information, not from AI. It shows the order of work: make the process transparent first, and an agent then handles the questions that remain.

Nudge students before deadlines

Missed deadlines cause more avoidable problems than almost anything else in academic operations: a bidding round missed, an exchange nomination not submitted, a placement application left incomplete. An agent can send personalised reminders based on what each student has and has not done yet, rather than one bulk email to everyone.

Guide registration choices

Elective bidding and course registration come with rules that students find hard to read: credit policies, concentration requirements, seat sharing between programmes, and how bid points work. An agent can explain these rules in plain language and in the context of the student's own record, and suggest study schedules that fit their term. For how bidding works, see what is elective course bidding?

The allocation itself should stay rule-based and transparent. An agent can explain why a student did or did not get a seat, but students must be able to trust that the allocation follows published rules.

Support career planning

Agents can look at a student's academic performance, activities and interests and suggest career paths and relevant internship opportunities that the student might not have considered. In placements, they can answer process questions, such as shortlist status, interview slots or offer rules, from the placement system's live data.

Flag students who may need support

Predictive tools can analyse data points such as class attendance, assignment submission timing, grade trajectories and engagement with the learning platform, and flag students who may be struggling. The agent's job is to alert an advisor early. The advisor's job is to decide what to do.

Why do AI agents depend on clean operational data?

An AI agent is only as good as the systems it reads from. If registration is in one spreadsheet, the timetable in another and placement schedules in email threads, the agent has nothing reliable to answer from, and it will either guess or give up. A wrong answer about a deadline or a credit rule is worse than no answer.

This is where most of the hard work sits, and it is the layer EDTEX works on. Registro and Skynet run registration, elective bidding, timetabling and placements as structured, rule-based processes, so the data an agent would need is accurate and up to date:

  • Registration and bidding: live rankings, clearing prices, allocations and programme-specific credit policies, all in one system.
  • Timetabling: at IIM Lucknow, clashes are detected in real time on a colour-coded dashboard that students and administrators can both see, and more than 90% of clashes were resolved automatically. Read the IIM Lucknow case study.
  • Placements: at ISB, Skynet delivered 50+ custom reports and analytics dashboards across 16+ automated placement workflows. Read the ISB case study.

Agents also need to integrate with the systems students already use: student portals, learning platforms, library databases, career services and administrative systems. The fewer separate systems there are, the simpler and safer that integration becomes.

What are the risks, and how should institutions manage them?

Data privacy and security

Student records are sensitive. Any agent that reads them must follow the same access rules as staff, collect only what it needs, and comply with data protection laws such as India's DPDP Act, 2023 and GDPR. Institutions should know where student data is processed and stored, and who can see the agent's conversations.

Human oversight

Agents should inform decisions, not make consequential ones on their own. Academic probation, exceptions to credit policy, disputes over allocation and anything to do with a student's wellbeing need a person. Every agent should make it easy to reach a human.

Bias

Recommendations built on past data can repeat past patterns. If previous cohorts from one background rarely chose a particular career track, an agent may quietly stop suggesting it. Review recommendations regularly and test them across different student groups.

Accuracy and upkeep

Policies, deadlines and course lists change every term. Agents must be updated with current information, and staff and faculty need training to work alongside them. Claims that agents can adapt to individual learning styles should be treated with care until an institution has tested them with its own students.

How should a business school start?

  • Fix the data first: put registration, timetabling and placements on systems of record before adding an agent on top.
  • Start with high-volume, low-risk questions: deadlines, process steps and policy explanations.
  • Keep a human in the loop: set clear rules for when the agent hands over to staff.
  • Measure the effect: track queries, response times and student satisfaction before and after.

Frequently asked questions

Will AI agents replace academic advisors or programme office staff?

No. They take routine questions off staff desks and flag students who may need help, so advisors can spend more time on the conversations that need judgement.

What data does an AI agent in a student system need?

Accurate, current data on registration, schedules, credit policies, deadlines and, for career support, placements. If that data is scattered across spreadsheets, fix that first.

Should AI agents allocate elective seats?

No. Allocation should follow transparent, published rules, such as a bidding mechanism. An agent can explain the outcome to the student.

How do AI agents comply with India's DPDP Act?

The institution remains responsible for the data. Agents should use role-based access, process only the data they need, and run on systems that comply with the DPDP Act, 2023 and, for international students, GDPR.

Conclusion

AI agents can make student support faster, more personal and available at any hour. The institutions that benefit most will be the ones that get the basics right first: clean data, transparent processes and people who stay in charge of the decisions that matter.
