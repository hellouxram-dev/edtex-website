Registro · Elective course bidding

Allocate elective seats fairly, by bid points, in rounds you control

Students spend bid points on the electives they value most. Registro runs demand estimation, bidding, waitlist and add/drop rounds under your credit rules, and shows students live seat availability and clearing bids.

EDTEX Registro Course Bidding allocates elective seats by bid points, under each institution’s own credit rules, across demand estimation, bidding, waitlist and add/drop rounds. First built for IIM Ahmedabad in 2016, it now runs at business schools, law schools and universities including IIM Bangalore, IIM Calcutta, ISB and O.P. Jindal Global University, where more than 5,000 students bid in a single real-time window.

What it does

High-demand electives should not go to whoever clicks fastest. Registro gives students the information to bid sensibly, and gives the programme office one governed workflow for every round.

Bidding with live information

Students see what a seat is likely to cost before they spend points.

  • Clearing bids and live demand for each course
  • Course outlines and prerequisites
  • Whether each bid is currently winning or losing, with the option to change it

Credit policies enforced as students bid

Your rules are checked by the system in every round, not by staff afterwards.

  • Minimum and maximum credits per student
  • Course prerequisites and timetable clashes
  • Programme-specific and specialisation-based credit rules

90% reduction in manual data validation checks for the programme office.

Better use of seats across programmes

For electives shared between programmes, registrars can plan and protect capacity.

  • Demand surveys before offerings are fixed
  • Dropping under-subscribed courses, then add/drop rounds with waitlists
  • Quotas for a primary programme’s seats in shared electives

Your own bidding rules

Registro is configured to your institution’s policy rather than a fixed model.

  • Point-based bidding or real-time open bidding
  • Preference-based allocation
  • Multiple rounds per term, trimester or semester

One workflow for the programme office

Upload cohorts and the elective master data, set credit and bid-point policies for the term, then open and close rounds, run add/drop and process drops in one place.

Reports for deans and committees

Dashboards on course demand, seat use and allocation outcomes, and reports for dean reviews, curriculum committees and accreditation, built on data from every cycle.

How a bidding cycle works for students

Each step happens in Registro, with the same rules applied to every student.

  1. ExploreBookmark preferred electives and read course outlines before spending a point.
  2. PrepareCheck timetable clashes and credit requirements, and answer the demand estimation survey.
  3. PracticeTake part in a full practice round, so the real one holds no surprises.
  4. BidBid in the final rounds with live clearing bids, seat availability and allocation status.
  5. AdjustUse add/drop and waitlist rounds to swap, drop or secure courses as seats move.
  6. ConfirmDownload the confirmed timetable for the term.

Questions about Registro Course Bidding

Short answers from our team. Anything else, ask us on the discovery call.

See all questions

What is course bidding in business schools?

Course bidding is a market-based way to allocate seats in popular electives. Each student receives a budget of bid points and bids on the courses they want; seats typically go to the highest bids up to each course’s capacity. Business schools use it because bids reveal how much students value each course, not just their ranked preferences.

What is the MRB in Registro course bidding?

MRB stands for Minimum Required Bid: the clearing price that wins a seat in a course at a given moment of an active round. The MRB can rise as demand for a course increases, or stay the same. It can be zero when fewer students bid for a course than there are seats, in which case a student may win a seat with zero bid points, unless the institution requires a minimum bid of one point.

What happens to bid points placed on a course a student loses?

On Registro, bid points placed on a course a student loses in a bidding round are reimbursed. Whether unused bid points carry forward to future terms or semesters is set by each institution’s bidding policy.

How does Registro keep the process fair and transparent?

Registro processes every bid with the same rules-based algorithm, configured to your institution’s bidding policy. Students see seat availability, clearing bids, prerequisite requirements and timetable clashes in real time, so allocations can be understood and verified rather than taken on trust. Winning seats are confirmed in the portal as soon as a round closes.

What happens if a student doesn’t get their preferred course?

Students who miss a preferred course can bid again in later rounds or enrol in alternative courses with available seats. How add/drop and waitlists work depends on the institution’s set-up: at NMIMS Mumbai, Registro runs several bidding and add/drop rounds each trimester, and waitlisted students are moved into available seats automatically.

Can Registro support our institution’s own bidding rules and credit policies?

Yes. Registro Course Bidding is configured to your institution’s rules, whether that means specific bid-point allocations, multiple bidding rounds or real-time open bidding. At O.P. Jindal Global University it enforces programme-specific credit policies, concentration requirements and seat-sharing rules across 10+ schools and 45+ programmes, with more than 5,000 students bidding in a single real-time window.

Can Registro run course bidding across multiple programmes?

Yes. At IIM Bangalore, Registro runs bidding across multiple degree programmes and for international exchange students in the same system, with cross-programme seat sharing handled by the platform.

Does Registro course bidding connect to add/drop and the timetable?

Yes. At IIM Calcutta, Registro course bidding and timetabling have run together since 2017, covering 21 term-level elective bidding cycles for more than 3,000 students, with structured add/drop rounds and clash-free timetables generated from bidding outcomes.

Can Registro forecast demand for future electives?

Yes. Registro runs demand estimation rounds, surveys in which students indicate interest in courses planned for future terms. Programme offices use the results to decide which electives to offer and how many sections to schedule before bidding opens.

How long does it take to deploy, and what support is provided?

Deployment typically takes between one and four weeks, depending on your programme requirements and academic policy; EDTEX configures the system to your credit rules, bidding model and course catalogue before go-live. Each bidding cycle is supported with video and document guides, live workshops and live chat support during active bidding windows, and students can write to registro@edtex.in during a round.
