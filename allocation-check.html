Self-check · 2 minutes · no sign-up

How fair and fast is your elective allocation?

Six questions about how your office runs elective allocation today. You get a score, the specific gaps worth looking at, and what institutions like yours did about them. Your answers stay in your browser.

0 of 6 answered

1 of 6How do students state their elective preferences today?
2 of 6How long from opening the round to publishing final allocations?
3 of 6When two students compete for the last seat, how is it decided?
4 of 6If a student appeals, can you show exactly why they got their allocation?
5 of 6How are timetable clashes between allocated courses found?
6 of 6After allocation, how are add/drop and swap requests handled?

What it measures

  • FairnessWhether every student is treated by the same written rule.
  • SpeedHow long the office is tied up by one round.
  • Audit trailWhether you can answer an appeal with evidence.
  • ClashesWhether students find timetable problems before you do.
  • After the roundWhether add/drop keeps the same rules.

    Who is the allocation self-check for?

    Programme office heads, chairs of programme committees and deans responsible for electives at business schools, law schools and universities. It takes about two minutes.

    Is the self-check anonymous?

    Yes. Your answers are scored in your browser and are not sent to EDTEX. If you choose to discuss the result, only what you type into the demo form is shared.

    How is the score calculated?

    Each of the six questions is scored from 0 to 3, giving a total out of 18 shown as a percentage. Any question scored 0 or 1 is listed as a gap, with a guide or case study that addresses it.

    Want to see your rules run on a real round?

    The first call is a 30 to 45 minute discovery session. We map how your office runs the process today, then show you EDTEX configured to your own rules.
